world of warcraft: forever · alliance classes, read against vanilla

Forever Class Codex

A Classic skeleton with more complete specs and less mandatory jank. Not Retail pacing.

Forever is not Vanilla with every spec swapped for its modern Retail version. Outdoor mobs are still meant to take 10–15 seconds to kill. Threat, crowd control, mana, weapon swings, positioning and tactical dungeon play all still matter.

What changed are the places where Vanilla classes were incomplete, awkward or too passive, or had specs that barely did the job they advertised. New abilities rarely turn a class into Wrath, Cata or modern rotational WoW. Mostly they add a meaningful action every few seconds, repair a broken resource loop, make a spec viable, or give the old toolkit a real capstone to revolve around.

System changes that touch every class

These change how every class feels before you look at a single talent.

Classic trees, more checkpoints

Rows still unlock progressively and the 31-point tree is intact. A new 16-point gold-medal milestone joins the 11, 21 and 31 breakpoints. Talents you used to buy just to make the class function, like Divine Spirit, Blessing of Kings and Improved Mark of the Wild–style effects, are now baseline. That frees points for actual decisions.

Dual spec at level 40

A huge lifestyle change for hybrids. Paladins, Priests, Druids and Shamans no longer pay the same respec tax for keeping a healing set and a solo set. Liking two specs of a class is now a good reason to pick it.

Merged hit and crit

Hit and crit are no longer split into physical and spell versions. Caster weapons carry real spell-power value, and healing power counts one-third as damage, so healing gear no longer makes solo play miserable.

Downranking curtailed

Spells far below your level get reduced benefit from spell power and lower proc chances. Blizzard's example is a level 60 Mage spamming Rank 1 Frostbolt, which no longer gets normal Frostbite behavior. Where the trick did something useful, they add a real spell instead, such as the Mage's Frost Blast.

Raid-wide utility

Late beta made Battle Shout, Trueshot Aura, Moonkin Aura, Leader of the Pack, and Shaman Grace of Air, Strength of Earth, Windfury and Flametongue all raid-wide. Salvation no longer competes with other Blessings. There is far less shuffling of five-player buff groups and stacking copies of a class to cover each subgroup.

Alliance-specific notes

Dwarf Shaman is now an Alliance option. Human Hunter and Gnome Priest are also new combinations. The new Skyborne race can be Warriors, Hunters, Rogues, Druids and Mages.

Weapon-skill racials are gone in the Oct 16 build. Human Sword Specialization became Ruthless (+2% crit) and Dwarf Mace Specialization became Earth Fury (+2% critical damage and healing). So Human is no longer the default melee race just because of swords.

Warrior

Arms · Fury · Protection

Distance from VanillaLow–moderate

verdict

Choose Warrior when you specifically don't want Forever to feel like another expansion's class design. You still manage rage, stances, weapon swings and threat. Forever mostly removes the places where Vanilla Warrior fought its own mechanics.

Late-beta ledger

Warrior

  • Tactical MasteryBaseline, lvl 14
  • Rage kept on stance swap10
  • Victory Rush (lvl 20)Heals 10% max HP
  • Retaliation / Shield Wall15 min, unshared
  • Battle ShoutParty Raid-wide
  • SlamImmune to pushback

The safest pick if you want Classic, just better. Of all nine Alliance classes, Warrior changes least in concept.

Baseline

Rage generation is the first big change. Rage from taking damage is calculated before armor and absorbs, so better gear or a Priest shield no longer starves your resource. Offensive rage is normalized much more tightly around weapon speed, and crits generate extra. Rage gets more predictable, but you still have to manage it.

Tactical Mastery is baseline at level 14 and keeps 10 rage through stance changes at first. Stance dancing stays, but you no longer spend deep Arms points just to make it pleasant. Victory Rush at level 20 heals for 10% of max health after a qualifying kill, so a low health bar no longer kills your leveling momentum.

Recklessness, Retaliation and Shield Wall no longer share a cooldown, and Retaliation and Shield Wall drop to 15 minutes. Rend and Thunder Clap scale with attack power, Thunder Clap works in Defensive Stance, and Sunder Armor threat scales with AP.

Arms

16Spearing Strike

Weaponmaster consolidates the old sword, axe and mace specializations. Axes and polearms favor crit, maces and staves get armor penetration, and swords keep their extra attacks. You are no longer locked to whichever weapon type you specced. Bloodthrill lets Rend enable Overpower, and Improved Slam no longer delays or interrupts your auto swing. Arms is now a deliberate weapon-master spec built around Overpower, Rend and Slam, not a wait for Mortal Strike.

Fury

16Raging Blows

Raging Blows improves Whirlwind's dual-wield interaction and makes Cleave cheaper. Dual Wield Specialization helps the off-hand hit problem, Unbridled Rage works better with two-handers, and Enrage can trigger from taking damage, not only from crits. Death Wish now costs you increased damage taken instead of armor and resistances. Bloodthirst loses its small heal and grants movement speed instead.

Protection

Still recognizable sword-and-board, with cleaner resource and threat systems. Shield Specialization gives more rage from blocks. Master of Defense generates rage from shield avoidance, Vanguard lets you Charge into Defensive Stance play, and Focused Rage trims ability costs. Shield Slam scales better with block value. Improved Shield Block and Improved Taunt functionality move toward baseline.

Paladin

Holy · Protection · Retribution

Distance from VanillaVery high

verdict

If you liked the idea of Vanilla Paladin but not what happened when you tanked or played Ret at 60, this is the clearest "play it again, it's different" pick. It is still slow, mana-hungry, seal-and-judgment Paladin, but all three specs finally have enough to work with.

Late-beta ledger

Paladin

  • ConsecrationBaseline, lvl 20
  • Consecration full effectFirst 4 targets
  • Righteous Fury Holy threat60% 90%
  • Champion of the Light (Int→SP)20/40/60% 10/20/30%
  • Reckoning chargesUp to 4
  • SalvationNo longer a Blessing slot

Consecration's spell power coefficient was also lowered significantly.

One of the biggest overhauls in the game. Paladin is built to be a real healer, tank or Retribution damage dealer.

Baseline

Paladins learn Holy Strike, an instant Holy weapon attack, early in leveling. More importantly, Judgment no longer consumes your Seal. That one change removes the judge-and-reseal routine that made Vanilla Paladin feel so passive.

Consecration is baseline at 20 instead of a Holy talent. Only the first four enemies take full damage and threat. Late tuning cut its coefficient and raised Righteous Fury's Holy threat from 60% to 90%, steering Prot toward deliberate tank threat instead of a scaling AoE bomb.

Reckoning keeps its Classic behavior. It stores up to four charges and releases them on your next autoattack, and instant attacks no longer reset your swing.

Protection

★Seal of Fury

Seal of Fury is the center of the spec. It adds Holy damage, favors fast attacks, and turns some of that into absorb shields while you wear a shield. Its Judgment taunts, so Alliance Paladins finally have a real taunt.

Talents build around it. The Seal's mitigation returns mana, Swift Judgment recovers Judgment faster, Iron Creed ties Holy Strike to mitigation, and Templar's Bulwark is a big absorb cooldown based on max health. Prot now has a tank seal, a taunt, baseline Consecration, stronger threat, real mitigation, mana sustain and a serious defensive cooldown. Five-man groups no longer have to work around a missing taunt.

Retribution

16Sacred Arbiter

Sacred Arbiter empowers Holy Strike and refreshes your Judgments. Champion of the Light converts Intellect into spell damage, cut late from 20/40/60% to 10/20/30%. Vindication now lowers the enemy's attack power and raises yours, and Instrument of Law improves Hammer of Wrath and threat.

Twist of Light makes seal twisting a talent. Instead of an accidental swing-timer trick, your previous Seal echoes into your next melee hit. Ret keeps seal twisting, but you learn it from the tree, not from a latency guide.

Holy

16Divine Favor31Light's Vigil

The smallest role change, but much more choice in which spell to cast. Voice of Truth resists silences and interrupts, Reverence improves in-combat Spirit regen, and Infusion of Light makes Holy Shock and Flash of Light crits speed up Holy Light. Holy Shock sits deeper in the tree on a much shorter cooldown than in Vanilla.

Light's Vigil is an expensive spell that resets Holy Shock, then adds a damage or party-heal payoff depending on the target. Holy now runs on Holy Shock instead of collapsing into rank-picked Flash of Light and Holy Light spam.

Hunter

Beast Mastery · Marksmanship · Survival

Distance from VanillaHigh

verdict

A Hunter that still follows old-school Hunter rules but has three genuinely different fantasies. Compared with Vanilla, Survival is close to a new spec.

Late-beta ledger

Hunter

  • Aimed ShotAll Hunters, lvl 20
  • Croc Dismember heal cut50% 25%
  • Bat Sonic Blast cast slowHalved
  • Trueshot AuraRaid-wide
  • Active traps1 Fire + 1 Ice
  • Auto Shot stand still0.5 sec

Dismember's duration and cooldown were also nerfed, and Bird of Prey's Mine! was restrained.

More changed than the ranged rotation suggests. The three trees are now different ways to play, not three ways to amplify the same ranged pet base.

Baseline

Aimed Shot is learned by every Hunter at 20. Arcane Shot no longer shares a cooldown with it; Multi-Shot does. Traps work in combat, and fire and ice traps run on separate cooldowns. The latest build fixed it so you can keep one Fire Trap and one Ice Trap active. Traps become part of combat, not just pull prep and Feign Death gymnastics.

Pets get one of the deepest redesigns. They inherit stats from your gear, every family gets basic tools such as Bite or Claw and Dash or Dive, and each family has its own utility ability. Blizzard keeps nerfing the strongest of those, so family choice matters more than in Vanilla, but don't count on early-beta pet utility surviving.

The Oct 16 build added an option to switch automatically between Auto Attack and Auto Shot. Auto Shot still needs half a second standing still, but it can fire during Aimed and Sniper Shot casts. Shot timing remains part of the class.

Beast Mastery

16Summon Hawk

Focused Fire and Summon Hawk, a temporary second attacker tied to your target, make BM more active. It isn't modern BM's constant zoo, but there is more going on than a bigger pet auto-attack.

Marksmanship

31Sniper Shot

Lone Wolf gives a large damage bonus when you play without a pet. Trueshot Aura moves deeper into MM and becomes raid-wide. Sniper Shot, the 31-point capstone, is a long-cast, extreme-range precision shot. If you only kept a pet because Vanilla forced you to, this spec explicitly supports leaving it behind.

Survival

★Melee hybrid

The dramatic one. Survival is no longer mostly a PvP and control talent collection. It is an intentional melee and ranged hybrid. Predator's Edge boosts melee crit damage and off-hand play, Expose Prey lets attacks on your marked target enable Mongoose Bite, Strider Kick is a weapon attack tied to movement, and Lacerating Strikes turns repeated Mongoose Bites into a bleed. Resourcefulness folds traps and crits from both ranges into the spec.

The Classic dead zone remains. Survival is designed to move through the ranges with useful actions on both sides, not to erase range management.

Rogue

Assassination · Combat · Subtlety

Distance from VanillaModerate

verdict

For someone who already likes Classic Rogue and wants better-defined trees. It is not the class to choose if you want something radically new.

Late-beta ledger

Rogue

  • Expose Armor= 5 Sunders
  • Expose Armor + SunderBoth can apply
  • Mutilate2 combo points
  • SapKeeps you stealthed
  • Poisons + stonesCoexist
  • One-handed axesUsable

Classic Rogue with clearer specs, not a reinvention. Energy, combo points, positionals, poisons, stealth and control are all kept.

Baseline

Poisons carry more charges and stack with sharpening and weight stones. Sap no longer breaks Stealth. Distract also lowers enemy stealth detection. Expose Armor equals five Sunders and no longer blocks Warriors from applying Sunder Armor, which ends an old raid-debuff conflict.

Blizzard's stated target is for Rogue to have the highest or among the highest single-target damage in a Patchwerk-style fight, trading utility and range for raw melee damage. That is a design goal, not proof of launch balance, so don't pick Rogue because it is supposed to top the meters.

Assassination

21Mutilate31Venom

The tree that changes most. It becomes the explicit dagger and poison spec. Mutilate hits with both daggers, does more to poisoned targets and awards two combo points. Venom is a finisher that buffs your poison damage and application. PvE play centers on keeping Slice and Dice and Venom up, with poisons as the core of the spec. Malice feeds poison crits, and Improved Poisons is about keeping the poison engine running.

Combat

Still the most recognizably Classic tree. Hack and Slash merges several weapon specializations, so a raid weapon drop is rarely the wrong type for your talents. Precision applies more broadly, including to poisons and spell effects, Backstab gets more support, and Flawless Execution lowers Eviscerate's energy cost so finishers flow better.

Subtlety

16Premeditation31Thousand Cuts

Still the stealth, setup and control tree, now with a clearer damage loop. Hemorrhage now boosts your own Rupture instead of acting mainly as a raid physical-damage debuff. Cutthroat lets Backstab open up Ambush-like attacks in combat, and Thousand Cuts makes Rupture ticks lower the energy cost of Hemorrhage and Backstab.

Priest

Discipline · Holy · Shadow

Distance from VanillaHigh

verdict

Pick Priest if you want to stay recognizably Classic but feel that your spec matters every time you play. Disc is the spec you will most need to relearn.

Late-beta ledger

Priest

  • Devouring Plague cooldown60 sec 24 sec
  • Shadow Word: Death cooldown12 sec 18 sec
  • Vampiric Embrace30 sec CD, unresistable
  • Devouring Contagion+2 targets
  • Fear Ward, Devouring PlagueAll races
  • LightwellRemoved

Many tools from later expansions, with Classic mana management and cast speed intact. All three specs become much more complete.

Baseline

Fear Ward and Devouring Plague are no longer race-locked, and every race still gets two racial Priest spells of its own. Divine Spirit and Shadow Word: Death are baseline. Prayer of Healing can target another party, several Priests can keep Renew on one target, and Fade drops more threat.

For the Alliance, Humans keep Feedback and Divine Grace–style options, Dwarves keep Desperate Prayer with Chastise-style utility, Night Elves have Starshards and Elune's Grace, and new Gnome Priests get their own racial spells. Race still matters for flavor, but raids no longer need you to be a Dwarf for Fear Ward.

Discipline

21Penance

The biggest change from Vanilla. Disc used to be the tree holding Power Infusion, Inner Focus, mana efficiency and some shields. Now it is a shield-healing and damage-weaving spec. Penance can damage or heal. Soul Warding improves repeated shields, Renewed Hope keys off Weakened Soul, and Divine Aegis turns direct-heal crits into absorbs.

Power in Light links Smite and Penance to targets carrying Holy Fire or Shadow Word: Pain, so you weave damage in on purpose, not just when healing is quiet. The Oct 16 build refined this further and moved Mental Strength earlier in the tree.

Holy

16Binding Heal31Prayer of Mending

Litany of Light rewards switching between different heals instead of repeating one. Prayer of Mending bounces between allies as you remember it. Lightwell is removed. Vanilla Holy had many good ranks but settled on the efficient few, and Forever rewards using the whole healing kit.

Shadow

Fixes famous Vanilla limits without becoming modern Shadow. Shadowform allows more non-healing spells and adds big mana efficiency and crit damage. Shadow Weaving is now a personal buff, so you're less likely to be brought just as the Warlocks' debuff Priest.

Vampiric Embrace becomes an activated 30-second cooldown. Devouring Contagion spreads Devouring Plague to two more targets, and Early Demise gives Shadow Word: Death an execute role. Dark Inspiration makes Mind Blast and fully channeled Mind Flays raise Spirit for your party, and Improved Mind Flay ramps damage on each tick, so your filler finally has a reason to finish the channel.

Shaman

Elemental · Enhancement · Restoration

Distance from VanillaVery high

verdict

For an Alliance veteran who wants something that still feels like old WoW but couldn't be played on Alliance before, Dwarf Shaman belongs high on the shortlist. You still place totems and think about them. You just stop spending half your globals rebuilding the same four.

Late-beta ledger

Shaman

  • Totem global cooldown1.5 sec 1 sec
  • Buff totem duration2 min 5 min
  • Chain Lightning manaAbout −35%
  • Grace of Air + Windfury/FTCoexist
  • Elemental MasteryBack at 31, 3 min
  • Mana TideNew rank at lvl 38

Fire Nova trades coefficient and base damage for a large mana cost cut.

The most novel Alliance choice by definition: Alliance couldn't roll a Shaman in Vanilla. It is also heavily redesigned.

Baseline

Even if you know Horde Vanilla Shaman well, this has one of the longest quality-of-life lists in the game. Totems use a one-second GCD, and long buff totems last five minutes. There is a dedicated totem interface. Weapon imbues last much longer and stack with temporary weapon enchants. Fire Nova is now a spell fired through your placed fire totem.

Totemic Recall refunds some mana and Totemic Projection moves your existing totems. Three presets, Call of the Elements, Call of the Spirits and Call of the Ancestors, drop a whole set at once, which removes most of the four-global totem piano.

As of Oct 16, Grace of Air can run alongside Windfury or Flametongue, though those two still exclude each other. Grace of Air, Strength of Earth, Windfury and Flametongue are all raid-wide.

Elemental

16Earthbound21Lava Burst31Elemental Mastery

Lightning Bolt and Chain Lightning have faster base casts than in Vanilla. An earlier deep dive dropped Elemental Mastery and put Lava Burst deeper in the tree. The Oct 16 build reversed that: Lava Burst moved up to 21 and Elemental Mastery returned at 31.

Elemental Mastery is a three-minute cooldown. For 15 seconds it shortens Lightning Bolt and Lava Burst casts and removes Chain Lightning's cooldown. Earthbound builds on Earthbind and roots, Lightning Overload keeps the proc identity, and Flame Shock duration feeds Lava Burst. That gives you a Flame Shock, Lava Burst, Lightning Bolt and Chain Lightning rotation with a burst window.

Enhancement

16Stormstrike

Mental Dexterity and Mental Quickness-style talents convert Intellect into attack power and spell or healing power. Improved Ghost Wolf works better indoors and can become instant. Stormstrike moves up to 16 points on an eight-second cooldown, and its debuff boosts your next Lightning Bolt, Chain Lightning or Earth Shock. Maelstrom Weapon speeds those casts as you fight, potentially to instant.

You keep the two-handed axe and mace fantasy. It isn't Retail's dual-wield-only Enhancement, but melee and spellcasting are now connected instead of sitting side by side. Spirit Weapons and Rockbiter give it some threat and defense, but Blizzard frames that as off-tanking or small-group tanking, not a fourth fully supported raid tank.

Restoration

16Mana Tide31Riptide

Water Shield restores mana and can now trigger from any Shaman spell crit, not just heal crits. Riptide is an instant heal plus HoT that strengthens Chain Heal on that target. With better Spirit regen while casting, Resto is about setting up Chain Heal and juggling several spells, not just spamming it.

Mage

Arcane · Fire · Frost

Distance from VanillaHigh

verdict

A strong pick if your Vanilla complaint was that the toolkit is great but raiding becomes one button. Forever's answer isn't Retail Mage. It's Classic Mage with an engine for each spec. Arcane in particular is a newly legitimate spec.

Late-beta ledger

Mage

  • Arcane Blast cast1.5 sec
  • Arcane Blast stack+15% Int as SP, ×4
  • Arcane Blast stack duration15 sec from first stack
  • Arcane Blast damage−40%
  • Ice Lance vs. frozen+300% +200%
  • Critical Mass (5 pts)10% crit
  • Fire PowerUp to 12%
  • Frost Blast (lvl 24)1.5 sec, 40% slow

The same Classic control chassis with much more developed damage specs. Blizzard adds rotation without replacing the utility and control class.

Baseline

Frostfire Bolt arrives at 40. It counts as both Fire and Frost, taking whichever resistance and damage interactions are better, and exists to make Fire/Frost hybrid builds legitimate.

Blizzard also goes after the most degenerate mass-control and farming patterns. Improved Blizzard slows less, and Cone of Cold's large-group control was toned down. The October build restored full Cone control on the first four targets, keeping small-pack power without letting Mages dominate huge mob trains.

Rank 1 Frostbolt is penalized, so Frost Blast arrives at 24: a 1.5-second Frost cast with a 40% slow that benefits from Frostbolt talents. It keeps the control Rank 1 Frostbolt used to provide and closes the downranking loophole.

Arcane

16Arcane Wrath

No longer a support tree for another school. It is a real damage spec. Each Arcane Blast grants spell damage equal to 15% of your Intellect, up to four stacks, and makes the next one cost more. Stacks last 15 seconds from the first application. Raw damage was cut 40% as part of that redesign.

That makes a Classic resource puzzle: ramp up a large spell-power state, then decide how hard to push it before the mana cost ramp hurts. Missile Barrage can proc from Arcane Missiles and moved much earlier. Arcane Wrath is a long cast that scales partly from maximum mana, so your mana pool becomes an offensive stat.

Fire

Much more proc-driven. Heating Up builds toward faster Pyroblasts: every Fire Blast adds a charge, and a Fire Blast crit adds two. Critical Mass reaches 10% crit at five points. Fire Power adds damage per Fire effect of yours on the target, up to 12%. Ignite is tracked per caster, ending the old shared-Ignite ownership mess. You stack Fire effects on a target and turn crits into Pyroblasts, instead of spamming Fireball, keeping Scorch up and hoping Ignite behaves.

Frost

Gets the most familiar later-WoW imports. Fingers of Frost lets attacks treat the target as frozen for Shatter, and Ice Lance hits much harder on frozen targets (+200%, down from +300%).

Winter's Chill is completely redesigned. It is no longer a stacking Frost crit debuff. Frostbolt, Frost Blast and Fingers-empowered Ice Lance build stacks, and your next Cone of Cold consumes them for much more damage. Cone becomes the finisher of a single-target or small-pack combo, not just an emergency slow.

Warlock

Affliction · Demonology · Destruction

Distance from VanillaHigh

verdict

Fewer headline fixes than Paladin or Druid, because Vanilla Warlock already worked, but the specs are much better defined. If you love the Classic Warlock toolbox but dislike how optimal PvE builds blur the trees together, this is a big improvement.

Late-beta ledger

Warlock

  • Shadow Mastery1–5% 2/4/6/8/10%
  • MaledictionReplaced by Grim Reach
  • Curse of Agony / DoomNow Banes
  • Curse of ShadowRemoved
  • Life TapScales with Spirit
  • Firestone / SpellstoneImbues, coexist with oils

The cleanest three-way spec split among the pure casters.

Baseline

Curse of Agony and Curse of Doom become Banes, a separate category from utility Curses, so your DoT no longer replaces the group's utility curse. Curse of the Elements now covers more magic schools, and Curse of Shadow is removed as redundant.

Firestones and Spellstones are weapon imbues that stack with oils. Life Tap scales with Spirit, tying caster gear directly to the health-to-mana engine. Drain Soul generates Soul Shards more reliably during the channel, and demons inherit more of your stats.

Affliction

31Wrack

A real DoT, crit and drain spec. Pandemic lets DoTs and drains crit, Soul Siphon rewards stacking your Affliction effects on a target, and Wrack is a channeled Shadow filler that amplifies the DoTs already running. Grim Reach extends Affliction range. Deep Affliction now has a reason to stay deep at endgame instead of being a utility stop before Shadow Bolt spam takes over.

Demonology

31Demonic Pact

Demonic Sacrifice moves much earlier, and Demonic Pact lets you keep one demon's sacrifice bonus while summoning and keeping another. That resolves the Vanilla joke that the famous Demo-adjacent playstyle was killing your demon for a buff. Decimation adds a Soul Fire execute, Demonic Brand improves pet threat and damage, and Soul Link sits in the deeper survivability package.

Destruction

16Conflagrate31Incinerate

Shadow and Fire casts now strengthen each other through new talents. Bane of Havoc marks a second target that takes part of the damage you deal elsewhere, adding deliberate two-target cleave. Incinerate hits much harder on targets with Immolate, giving Fire Destro a real filler instead of a set of talents around Shadow Bolt and Conflagrate.

Druid

Balance · Feral · Restoration

Distance from VanillaVery high

verdict

If you know Classic deeply, you will notice the changes constantly. Weapon upgrades matter in forms, powershifting is formalized, Bear has better threat tools, Balance has a spell cycle and Resto gets core healing tools. It is still Vanilla Druid, minus a remarkable number of its structural oddities.

Late-beta ledger

Druid

  • Omen of ClarityBaseline, lvl 20
  • LacerateBaseline, lvl 42
  • Tiger's FuryRemoved Shifting Power
  • Furor, Cat → Bear → CatEnergy kept
  • Moonkin AuraRaid-wide, spell crit
  • BarkskinUsable in forms

Energy does not regenerate while in Bear Form.

Perhaps the deepest Classic-plus redesign. The changes reach the engine level.

Baseline

Shapeshifted melee damage scales with your weapon's DPS, while forms keep fixed attack speeds. That ends the Vanilla assumption that a Feral weapon is a stat stick and that oddities like Manual Crowd Pummeler define the spec. Better weapons now mean better Cat and Bear damage, and weapon effects can work in forms.

Forms can use more objects, consumables and racials, and Barkskin works while shifted. Entangling Roots works indoors, Nature's Grasp is baseline early, Omen of Clarity is baseline at 20, and Druids finally get an out-of-combat Revive. Bear gets Lacerate at 42 for stacking bleed and threat, and Swipe scales with AP. Forms are immune to Disarm once you're shifted, but a Disarm before you shift still hurts.

Balance

16Insect Swarm

Built around a new Eclipse, not the modern self-oscillating bar. Casting Wrath shortens your next Starfires, giving you a reason to alternate the two nukes. Nature's Splendor extends your effects, and Moonkin Form allows more non-healing spells. Omen of Clarity does more for casters in Moonkin, and Moonglow helps mana efficiency. It is no longer a Starfire/Wrath caster with chronic mana problems and a novelty aura. It's still slow-cast Classic Balance, with no Retail Astral Power.

Feral

★Shifting Power↓Berserk

The most fascinating change if you understand Classic powershifting. Tiger's Fury is gone, replaced by Shifting Power, which converts a chunk of base mana into Energy on a cooldown. Mana stays a secondary resource you spend for Cat damage, but as a deliberate talent, not by canceling and re-entering Cat Form with Wolfshead Helm and Furor.

Claw, Shred and Rake scale better, and Primal Bite becomes a key active attack. Berserk, deep in the tree, removes Primal Bite's cooldown, makes it hit several enemies and greatly raises your combo-point builders' crit chance. The rhythm itself changes. Blizzard took powershifting's core resource-conversion idea and made it supported design.

Restoration

16Swiftmend31Wild Growth

Swiftmend no longer consumes the HoT it uses, a big usability win. Wild Growth heals several party or group members over time. Gift of the Earthmother speeds up the global cooldown on Rejuvenation, Swiftmend and Wild Growth. Mark of the Wild upgrades no longer eat premium talent points, and Spirit regen is better. Resto keeps its Classic HoT and Healing Touch identity and gains a real instant reactive heal and a sustained group HoT.

for the veteran

Choose your path

You know what a Warrior is. The useful question is which familiar class gives you an interesting Forever experience.

The most improved version of a class you know

The new design changes what you do often enough that you'll keep thinking "oh, this is Forever": Prot's taunt and mitigation, Ret's Holy Strike and Seals, Druid forms and Shifting Power, Shaman totems, melee Survival, Penance Disc and the Arcane engine.

Vanilla feel, rough edges sanded off

Warrior still revolves around rage, swings, stances and threat. Rogue still revolves around energy, combo points, poisons, positionals and stealth. The changes fix friction and make the trees less arbitrary instead of inventing new combat models.

WarriorRogue

You hated the hybrid tax

Prot Paladin has a real taunt and baseline AoE tanking. Feral weapon scaling and supported energy conversion fix long-standing oddities, Balance gets a spell loop, and Ret becomes an intentionally supported damage spec.

PaladinDruid

The single most novel Alliance experience

A whole class Vanilla Alliance never had, and not just Horde Shaman moved to the Alliance side. Five-minute totems, one-second totem globals, Call presets, Projection, Recall, raid-wide buffs, Lava Burst, Maelstrom Weapon and Riptide make a much richer Shaman.

Dwarf Shaman

Survival should mean the Hunter who fights up close

Strider Kick, Mongoose Bite interactions, melee crit and off-hand talents and traps make it probably the biggest identity break among pure DPS. You can still dual-spec into petless Lone Wolf MM or pet-heavy BM.

Hunter

Priest healing trees felt the same

Disc may be the biggest sleeper pick. Penance, Divine Aegis, better shields and damage weaving make it a real spec. Holy gets Binding Heal, Prayer of Mending and rewards for alternating spells.

the conclusion

Don't ask which Vanilla class you already like most. Ask which one had a fantasy you liked but mechanics you wished Blizzard had finished.

Forever's class work is aimed overwhelmingly at that second question. Paladin, Druid and Shaman are the most transformed. Hunter, Priest, Mage and Warlock gain distinct spec loops. Warrior and Rogue stay closest to refined Classic originals.

Final raid DPS, the tank hierarchy and healing throughput are the things most likely to move. In mid-October Blizzard was still changing coefficients, talent positions, raid buffs and cooldowns. The same late build redesigned Arcane Blast, moved Lava Burst, restored Elemental Mastery, cut Champion of the Light, changed Paladin threat, nerfed Hunter pet abilities, adjusted Ice Lance and revised Shadow talents. Choose by mechanical identity, not by an October beta damage ranking.
